# Hall and Puja Brainstorm — Round 1

These are low-detail concept studies. The architect plan remains the binding geometry reference.

## Fixed planning envelope

- Living hall: 12 ft 6 in × 18 ft 1.5 in.
- L-sofa: approximately 11 ft main run × 4 ft 6 in return, 34–36 in deep.
- Sofa seat: one continuous bench, with no seat divisions.
- Sofa service board: 25–26 in above finished floor, below the approximately 30 in sofa back.
- Board services: charging module at each accessible end, each with Indian 6A socket, USB-A and USB-C; dimmable 2700K concealed wall-wash LED.
- TV: 75 in diagonal, approximately 65.4 in wide × 36.8 in high.
- TV console: approximately 8 ft 6 in wide × 14–15 in deep, with ventilated removable service bays, cable raceway and IR repeater.
- Pooja unit: 42 in wide × maximum 13.5 in deep × approximately 84 in high.
- Pooja inventory: seven idols up to 4 in high and eight framed photos, including one 9 in high frame and smaller 3 in frames.
- No dining table and no sink/handwash in the hall.

## Dimensional fit check

Across the 150 in hall width, a 36 in sofa plus a 15 in TV console leaves 99 in between their fronts. With a 20 in-deep coffee table, this permits about 39.5 in clear on each side, exceeding the 36 in target circulation.

The approximately 118 in solid TV-wall portion can accept the 65.4 in-wide television and an approximately 102 in console without overlapping the window or doorway zone.

Within the 42 in puja width, an approximately 38 in clear internal width accepts four photo frames per rail and seven small idols on a two-level 34–36 in-wide removable riser. The 13.5 in depth accepts a 9–10 in-deep pull-out diya tray while remaining inside the architect footprint.

## Living-room options

### L1 — Minimal warm walnut

References emphasized: 6, 68, 78 and 99. Thin pale TV panel, restrained vertical slats, slim walnut console and simple recessed ceiling lights. Lowest visual weight and easiest to maintain.

### L2 — Layered stone and wood

References emphasized: 2, 55, 57 and 81. Larger stone-look TV field, controlled fluted walnut bands, one lit niche and richer indirect lighting. Most premium-looking, but requires careful stone-joint and lighting coordination.

### L3 — Terracotta organic

References emphasized: 23, 36, 61, 81 and 99. Muted terracotta arch, light geometric veneer lines and warmer organic accents. Strongest personality without using a bulky honeycomb installation.

## Puja options

### P1 — Four-panel folding fluted glass

References emphasized: 28, 57 and 64. Four narrow panels fold back against the sides. The two photo rails hold four frames each; seven idols sit on a removable two-level stone riser.

### P2 — Top-down metal tambour

References emphasized: 23, 50 and 88. The visual idea of reference 88 is retained, but combustible cloth is replaced by micro-perforated brass-finish aluminium. It rolls into the top pelmet and therefore uses no floor or door-swing space.

### P3 — Sliding fluted-glass/jali panels

References emphasized: 26, 27, 29, 35, 55, 56 and 62. Two slim panels bypass or pocket at the sides. This is the least intrusive closure near the bedroom door and the easiest option to operate daily.

## Real-flame safety requirements

- Use quartz, sintered stone or another non-combustible liner at the altar, rear panel and overhead hot zone.
- Keep the pull-out diya tray fully extended whenever the flame is lit.
- Keep at least 18 in clear above the flame and locate LEDs outside the hot zone.
- Provide permanent low and high ventilation that cannot be blocked by the closure.
- Never close any door, slider or tambour while the diya, incense or dhoop is burning.
- Keep wood edges and photo frames outside the direct flame and smoke plume.

## Recommendation for the next round

L1 is the safest base for the compact hall; selected L2 materials can be added without making the room heavy. For the mandir, P2 uses the least circulation space, while P3 is mechanically simpler and easier to service. P1 is visually attractive but should proceed only if the folded panels can be detailed to remain completely outside the nearby door path.
